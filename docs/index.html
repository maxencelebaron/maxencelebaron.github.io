Pako Maxence Tekou
Applied Mathematics Researcher

I am pursuing a PhD in Applied Mathematics and Computer Science within the GHOST team, a joint research team of Inria, Université Grenoble Alpes, and CNRS. My research focuses on Online Learning, Reinforcement Learning, and Optimization, with an emphasis on policy gradient methods, under the supervision of Dorian Baudry and Bruno Gaujal. Prior to my PhD, I completed two years of engineering studies at ENSAI, a French engineering school offering multidisciplinary training in applied mathematics and statistics, computer science (machine learning and AI), and economics. I then pursued the MVA Master's degree at ENS Paris-Saclay, a renowned research-oriented master's program in applied mathematics and AI. Before joining ENSAI, I grew up in Togo, where I played basketball at the national level during high school, before moving to France to undertake two years of intensive training in mathematics, physics, and computer science, preparing for the highly competitive entrance examinations to France's leading engineering schools. In practice, my goal is to develop new algorithms for reinforcement learning and sequential decision-making. I am particularly interested in understanding the theoretical properties of learning algorithms and using these insights to guide algorithm design. My background in machine learning also motivates my interest in combining modern deep learning methods with theoretical approaches to reinforcement learning. In this context, I am also interested in Bayesian methods, which provide a natural framework for quantifying uncertainty and leveraging this uncertainty for efficient exploration. I find it useful to study simpler settings before addressing the full complexity of reinforcement learning. In particular, bandit problems provide a tractable framework for studying fundamental questions such as convergence, regret guarantees, stability, and learning dynamics. The goal is then to determine which of these insights can be transferred to more general Markov decision processes and used to develop new reinforcement learning algorithms. Core research interests Online Learning and Learning Theory Multi-Armed Bandit Theory (Deep) Reinforcement Learning Machine Learning and Deep Learning, with a particular interest in theory and Representation Learning Optimization and Online Convex Optimization Statistics Bayesian Machine Learning Broader research interests Structured Data: Time Series and Graphs. My interest in time series developed during my studies at ENSAI, particularly through coursework in economics and finance. Beyond classical methods for time series analysis, I am also interested in the application of online learning to sequentially evolving data. I am also interested in machine learning on graphs, particularly in the context of bandit problems, where graph structure can be incorporated into the learning process. Graph Signal Processing. My interest in Graph Signal Processing naturally stems from my broader interest in structured data, particularly time series and graphs. I find it especially interesting as a framework for studying signals defined over graph-structured data, and for its connections with machine learning on graphs and reinforcement learning. In particular, I am interested in applications involving dynamic graphs and the use of graph-based representations in reinforcement learning, including real-world and industrial-scale problems. Stochastic Calculus and Bayesian Methods. My background in statistics led me to develop a strong interest in probability and stochastic processes. This naturally connects to Stochastic Calculus, Stochastic Approximation, and MCMC, which also provide useful tools for Bayesian inference, optimization, and the analysis of learning algorithms.

Research Interests

My research focuses on the theoretical foundations and practical algorithms for deep learning, with emphasis on:

  • Neural Architecture Growth & Optimization: Principled criteria for expanding network capacity based on expressivity bottlenecks
  • Reinforcement Learning Theory: Policy optimization, plasticity, and sample efficiency in deep RL
  • Convex Optimization: Gradient methods and convergence analysis
  • Statistical Learning Theory: Generalization, regularization, and the interplay between optimization and statistics
